As it happened

Bedulu & the Wood School — first impressions

Settling into Bedulu and the school rhythm.
New home

Bedulu, Wood School and finding a house

In Bali we were immediately blown away by the warmth and kindness of the people. We found a small villa called "Bedulu Boutique Villas" overlooking rice fields after many viewings. Noam and Lyan started at Wood School Bali, a tiny international school of around fifty pupils set in nature with small huts as classrooms and dogs on campus. They love their teachers — Ibu Ara and Pak Yuki for Noam, and Ibu Didi (a Buddhist monk) for Lyan — and their days include meditation, morning yoga, songs, swimming, plant lessons, science projects and cooking classes.

School mornings, parents' cafe and new routines.
School life

Wood School — small, curious, joyful

The school has a parent cafe where Noam and Pablo can relax with coffee during school hours. Noam enjoys meditation and yoga, while Lyan sings every morning. They've already had swimming lessons, plant workshops, science projects and cooking classes. We even joined the Indonesian Independence Day celebrations on 17 August — games, music and a wonderful sense of community.

Coffee, rice fields and learning to get around.
Exploring

Finding local favourites

We explored rice fields, discovered great coffee shops and even visited a coffee plantation. Getting around on foot can be tricky, so we rented a small scooter which made house hunting and errands much easier.

More of the school days, routines and projects.
School days

Nature, routine and daily rhythm

Noam and Lyan are both deeply engaged in the school environment — from classroom life to outdoor learning and all the little rituals of the day.

Temple architecture, waterfalls and family outings.
Around Bali

Temple architecture and waterfalls

We love Bali's temple architecture and the way every outing feels both beautiful and grounded. The landscape gives us a constant sense of wonder.

Villa life, school assembly and independence celebrations.
Celebrations

Independence Day and new routines

The family has settled into a beautiful rhythm here, and the school celebrations made the arrival in Bali feel especially joyful.

Short stay

Singapore — wet border crossing and bright city lights

From Pulau Besar we returned to Mersing, drove to Johor Bahru, and crossed the border into Singapore on foot and by bus in heavy rain — an adventurous, soggy passage. In Singapore we stayed in an incredible hotel room with sweeping city views, ate at hawker centres and Sushiro (a favourite for Noam and Lyan), enjoyed the Gardens by the Bay evening symphony, walked through parks, and savoured the city until our flight to Bali departed.

Food stops, park moments and a favourite sushi run.
City life

Lovely food and lots of walking

Singapore gave us some of the best food moments of the trip and plenty of easy city wandering — especially around parks, food halls and late-night snacks.

→ Island stop

Pulau Besar, in the moment

A bumpy crossing and then a small, restful island.
Island life

Pulau Besar — rocky crossing, gentle days

Pulau Besar is a fantastic remote island off the coast of Mersing reached by a very rocky boat ride. We spent long, lazy hours on the beach relaxing, enjoyed excellent seafood, went for walks along the shore, played with the friendly cats, and met warm local people who made the visit special.

Hammocks, seafood and slow afternoons.
Relax

Slow days and local hospitality

Evenings were for fresh grilled fish and simple conversations with locals. The island's easy pace and friendly community made it a highlight of the Malaysia leg.

A few more quiet moments from the island.
Last light

Small island, big feeling

We kept making time to stop, rest, and simply be there — a rare calm in the middle of travel.
